Snip the Sprout: Gardening the Subconscious

If you plant a tomato seed, you do not get a rose. Elsie Lincoln Benedict builds a whole practice on that plain fact in How to Get Anything You Want, and it turns out to be the most hands-on idea in the book. She calls the subconscious a garden, governed by the Law of the Seed: every seed brings forth fruit of its own nature. Every feeling you plant is a seed. And a seed does not negotiate — it grows into the exact thing it was, no matter how much you wish it had become something nicer. Plant resentment and you do not harvest peace. Plant the quiet feeling that you always get cheated, and you grow a life full of evidence that you do. ...

The Genius Is Just the One Who Can't Be Kept Away

Genius is not a gift you were born with or denied. Elsie Lincoln Benedict strips the mystique off it completely in How to Get Anything You Want, and what is left is almost too simple to argue with. The genius, she says, is the one who cannot be kept away from the work. Not the most talented. Not the most credentialed. The one who forgets to eat, who treats the toil as play, who you would have to physically drag from the thing. The difference between the genius and the failure, she writes, is less in their abilities than in their attitude toward those abilities — the genius encourages the want, the failure inhibits it. ...
