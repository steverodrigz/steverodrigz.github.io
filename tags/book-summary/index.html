Book Summary

The Genius Is Just the One Who Can't Be Kept Away

Genius is not a gift you were born with or denied. Elsie Lincoln Benedict strips the mystique off it completely in How to Get Anything You Want, and what is left is almost too simple to argue with. The genius, she says, is the one who cannot be kept away from the work. Not the most talented. Not the most credentialed. The one who forgets to eat, who treats the toil as play, who you would have to physically drag from the thing. The difference between the genius and the failure, she writes, is less in their abilities than in their attitude toward those abilities — the genius encourages the want, the failure inhibits it. ...

Turn the Blocked Impulse Into a Bonfire

Don't try to kill the feeling. You cannot, and the attempt is what does the damage. Elsie Lincoln Benedict states the law flatly in How to Get Anything You Want: every intense impulse refused a natural outlet will find an abnormal one. The energy does not vanish when you sit on it. It goes underground, ferments, and comes back up somewhere uglier than where it started. You know the shape of this. The anger you swallowed in the meeting comes out sideways at home. The ambition you were told to tamp down turns into a low, chronic resentment. The grief you refused to feel shows up as a stiff neck and a short fuse. The impulse was looking for a door. You bolted the front one, so it took a window. ...

Watch the Feeling, Not the Thought

Stop trusting what you tell yourself you believe. Elsie Lincoln Benedict says the thing plainly in How to Get Anything You Want: the feeling, not the thought, is your predominant attitude toward any person or task. Your real position on a thing is not the position you reasoned your way to. It is the one that shows up in your chest before reason arrives. You can think you want the big project.
