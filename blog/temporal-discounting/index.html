Your Brain Is a Bad Time Traveler

We shrink the future to feed the present. A short look at the bias that quietly runs your worst choices.
May 24, 2026 · 1 min · Steven A. Rodríguez

Your brain is a terrible time traveler.

The Exponential Individuals Playbook names the bug: temporal discounting. We shrink the future down to almost nothing so the present can have what it wants right now. Tomorrow's version of you is a stranger, and we are not in the habit of doing favors for strangers.

You can watch it run in real time. The snooze button. The cart at checkout. The "I'll start Monday" that has now been said about forty Mondays. Each one is the present striking a deal at the future's expense, and the future is not in the room to object.

The fix is to drag the future into the room. Make tomorrow's you concrete — a face, a feeling, a specific morning that the present choice either rescues or wrecks. The stranger becomes a person you would not want to short. The deal gets harder to sign.

You will never out-argue the impulse in the moment. So set the moment up in advance, when the future still feels real.

Decide for tomorrow's you while you can still see them.
